# AFL beverage cooler — model and runtime V1

## Status

The model is registered as `apocalypse_firstlight:beverage_cooler`, a four-cell
2×1×2 block with two independently opening doors. Right-clicking a door region
plays its 0.40-second animation; committed BlockState, collision and rendered
resting pose change after eight server ticks. There is no inventory, drink
display/item interaction, GUI, refrigeration, emitted light, continuous sound,
or worldgen/building-NBT change. The blue header and interior diffusers are
material treatments only.

452 cubes, 23 groups, no meshes. Closed bounds: X=-8..24, Y=0..32, Z=-8..8 units

The bottom feet touch Y=0; handles remain inside the closed footprint.

## Organization

`beverage_cooler_root` owns `cabinet_body`, `top_sign_box`, `bottom_vent_base`,
`interior_shell`, `shelf_01` through `shelf_05`, `display_row_01` through
`display_row_05`, `left_door` and `right_door`.

Each door owns its corresponding `*_door_frame`, `*_door_glass`, and `*_handle`.
The row groups are empty alignment markers, not implemented display slots.
No beverages or branding are baked into the cabinet model.

## Independent door animation V1

- Left/right mean the viewer's left/right while facing the NORTH-facing front.
  The original door subtree labels were reversed from this viewpoint and have
  been corrected, including their frame/glass/handle descendants. UUID hierarchy
  and closed geometry are preserved.
- `left_door` hinge pivot: [22.87,16,-7.495]; opens to source Y=-95 degrees.
- `right_door` hinge pivot: [-6.87,16,-7.495]; opens to source Y=+95 degrees.
- `left_door_open`, `left_door_close`, `right_door_open`, `right_door_close`:
  each exactly 0.40 seconds, rotation-only, one bone track per clip, override=false.
- All four use Bezier interpolation and hold their final pose. Opening eases
  in and out; closing begins at a moderate speed and decelerates to exact zero.
  There is no overshoot, bounce, position/scale channel or sound event.
- The old three 0.95-second/100-degree preview clips are superseded and removed
  from the active source. Closed pose is zero rotation. Verify axis conversion
  and preserve/bake Bezier curves in the runtime export. The current exporter
  samples each cubic at 0.05-second intervals and retains the exact endpoints.
- Open doors extend beyond the closed block footprint; future placement and
  collision decisions must account for their swing outside the cabinet front.

## Runtime V1

- `BeverageCoolerBlock` uses `FACING` (NORTH/SOUTH/EAST/WEST), `PART`
  (`LOWER_LEFT`, `LOWER_RIGHT`, `UPPER_LEFT`, `UPPER_RIGHT`), `LEFT_OPEN`, and
  `RIGHT_OPEN`. All four cells hold identical direction and committed door
  booleans. The lower-left cell is master and the only BlockEntity/door-runtime owner;
  the lower-right cell is one step counterclockwise from `FACING` and both
  upper cells sit directly above their lower cells. Here left/right mean the
  player's left/right while facing the cabinet front.
- Placement checks all four positions, both supporting floor cells, chunk and
  world bounds, permissions, replaceability, fluids and obstruction before the
  `BeverageCoolerBlockItem` writes all four cells. Failed writes roll back.
  The source front is NORTH; the GeckoLib renderer rotates around the master
  and translates half a block toward the other cell. Runtime-export X mirroring
  and inverted Y door angles keep the authored left/right names and door-swing
  direction aligned with the physical `PART` and hit regions in every facing.
- Server `use` converts the hit point to source X/Y/Z. The thin closed front
  door envelopes span source X=8.05..23.1 (left) / -7.1..7.95 (right),
  Y=3.45..28.9 and Z=-8.25..-6.75. When open, the click target also follows
  the side-swing envelope near each outer hinge, reaching Z=-23.5. While a door
  is open, its hinge also has a small selectable area within the original
  footprint (through source Z=-4). Vanilla block targeting cannot reliably
  select the rest of a leaf projecting into an air cell. Client right-click
  therefore explicitly ray-tests the cached open-door-only shapes, cancels
  vanilla item use when a leaf is nearer than obstructing blocks, and sends
  one C2S request. The server repeats reach, open-leaf and occlusion checks
  before forwarding the hit to the existing master interaction. This is the
  only new packet, appended under shared network protocol 24. The casing and
  shelves remain no-op; there are no drink/item interactions.
- The master BlockEntity registers a controller per door, with non-overlapping
  animation bones. A pending flag rejects repeat clicks on that same door; the
  other door may start concurrently. The server schedules an eight-tick
  completion and then updates the relevant BooleanProperty on all four parts.
  Transition flags are transient; only the committed block states persist.
  A chunk reloaded during a transition returns to its last committed pose;
  after a completed transition, each controller's static pose reflects the
  saved booleans without replaying an opening animation.
- Collision/selection are cached per part × facing × two committed flags.
  Cabinet pieces cover side walls, rear wall, base/vent, top/sign and simplified
  shelf decks. Each closed glass leaf has its own thin front shape, while each
  open leaf has an approximately 95-degree, thin side-swing shape that extends
  forward. Open-door selection includes the in-footprint hinge proxy; collision
  does not, so it cannot reintroduce a front air wall. The opening/closing
  animation uses the *previous committed* shape until the eight-tick
  completion; no per-frame VoxelShape rebuild occurs.
- The block-entity renderer now writes opaque metal/interior bones to the
  cutout buffer and only the two `*_door_glass` bones to the translucent
  buffer. This preserves glass alpha without sorting the entire cabinet as
  one translucent object when viewed from very close range. Source geometry,
  UVs, animation and texture bytes were not changed by this rendering fix.
- Breaking any part removes all four. The selected/exploded cell evaluates
  the single cooler loot table; its three peers are removed without loot.
  Creative removal drops none. Explosion cannot drop all four parts because
  the first removed cell immediately tears down its peers. Mining requires a
  pickaxe and iron tier (`requiresCorrectToolForDrops`, both Minecraft tags),
  consistent with current commercial cabinets and the existing commercial door.
  The block uses metal hit/break sound; no custom continuous or door sound was
  added. Its particle fallback is vanilla black concrete rather than a missing
  texture.

Future `Beverage Cooler Display Storage V1` rule, **not implemented**: both
committed doors closed means all future display slots inaccessible; either
committed door open permits access to all future slots. There is no left/right
inventory partition.

### Rollback and verification

The source was untracked in Git at the start of this revision. A full embedded
pre-edit snapshot (including the user's live positioning) was saved to
`src/main/blockbench/backups/afl_beverage_cooler_pre_independent_doors.bbmodel`.
No Git commit was created; the snapshot provides file-level rollback.

Blockbench interpolation samples and all four combined endpoint states were
checked: [-95,95], [-95,0], [0,95], [0,0] in [left,right] order. Individual
clips leave the other door at rest. Single-left, single-right and both-open
views were inspected; frame, glass/glints and handle follow their door.
Geometry, UV and texture preservation are checked against the live pre-edit
snapshot. Additional previews: `src/main/blockbench/previews/afl_beverage_cooler_left_open.png`
and `src/main/blockbench/previews/afl_beverage_cooler_right_open.png`.

## Materials and checks

Charcoal metal casing, subtly lighter edges, gray/cool-white interior and wire
shelves, transparent blue-tinted glass, and an unlettered blue lightbox.
The atlas contains both opaque and translucent regions. Future game integration
must preserve PNG alpha and provide an appropriate glass rendering path;
opaque-only rendering would obscure the shelves.

Closed/front, oblique and fully opened views were inspected in Blockbench at
the model stage. The model and embedded/external texture bindings were checked.

For runtime V1, `processResources`, `compileJava` and `build` pass. The
isolated development GameTest (`src/dev/beverage-cooler-gametest.init.gradle`,
`src/dev/java/com/antaurora/apofirstlight/dev/BeverageCoolerGameTests.java`)
passes all four FACINGs, four door combinations per facing, independent and
concurrent transitions, delayed state/shape commit, collision sample points,
four part-mining positions with one drop each, creative removal without drops,
explosion cleanup without duplicate drops, blocked placement, iron-tier
harvest, and separate open-door selection from collision. In the graphical
development client, the cabinet/texture rendered, the left door opened alone,
the right door then opened alone, and the left-only and both-open poses were
restored after save/exit/re-entry. This visual check exposed and prompted the
runtime X-mirror correction. Each door was then closed separately by clicking
the corresponding in-footprint hinge, with the other door's state unaffected.
A 398-block teleport away and back restored the right-open pose and both
committed flags; this is a practical chunk-distance roundtrip, although actual
chunk unloading was not instrumented independently. NORTH, SOUTH, EAST and
WEST cabinets were placed in the graphical client; each rendered its front,
glass and shelves, and F3 confirmed its respective `facing` state. The
automated GameTest additionally covered all four orientations' part placement,
collision shapes and four committed door-state combinations. The detailed
single-/dual-door animation and hinge-click visual checks were performed on
the SOUTH-facing sample rather than repeated for every orientation.
