# Plants

<StatusTag s="test" /> AFL replaces vanilla grass, flowers, ferns and sugar cane with its own North American plants. Every plant is three-dimensional: a block of ground holds a small stand of one species, with stems, leaves and flowers; the wind sways them, people push through them, and footsteps and wheels flatten them

## Fourteen species

| Plant | Mostly grows in | Height | Looks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kentucky bluegrass | Grassland, roadsides, alpine meadows | 0.2–0.6 m | Soft fine turf with pale little panicles |
| Big bluestem | Temperate grassland, wet prairie, plateau steppe | Leaves about 0.6 m, culms 1.2–1.9 m | A tall bunchgrass whose dark three-forked heads are known as "turkey feet" |
| Little bluestem | Drier grassland, coastal sand, ridge-top oak woods, plateaus, badlands | 0.45–0.75 m | Blue-green clumps, copper-red upper culms, silvery seed heads |
| Pennsylvania sedge | Under trees | 0.15–0.3 m | Fine arching leaves spreading into a low cushion |
| Bracken fern | Under trees, coastal pine-oak woods | 0.3–0.7 m | A stiff stalk holding a broad triangular frond nearly level |
| Broadleaf plantain | Trodden ground, roadsides, barrens | Leaves on the ground, spikes 0.12–0.28 m | A flat rosette of broad oval leaves with arched veins, slender spikes |
| Common dandelion | Grassland, barrens, alpine meadows | 0.1–0.3 m | Deeply cut leaves lying flat, yellow flower heads and white seed clocks |
| Purple coneflower | Grassland, wet prairie, woodland | 0.6–1 m | A spiny orange-brown cone with drooping pink-purple rays |
| Black-eyed Susan | Grassland, woodland, coastal sand | 0.35–0.7 m | A dark brown dome with golden rays |
| Canada goldenrod | Grassland, wet prairie, riverbanks, barrens | 1–1.5 m | Colonies of upright leafy stems topped by yellow panicles |
| Common yarrow | Grassland, woodland, alpine meadows | 0.3–0.7 m | Feathery leaves and flat white flower clusters |
| Broadleaf cattail | Water's edge, wetlands | 1.5–2.3 m | Long sword-shaped leaves and dark brown cylindrical spikes |
| Common reed | Water's edge, tidal marsh, lake fens | 2–3 m | Tall hollow culms, long leaves held out and drooping, purple-brown feathery plumes |
| Allegheny blackberry | Woodland edges | 1–1.5 m | Thorny arching canes, palmate leaves, clusters of black drupes (a few still red) |

- Broadleaf cattail and common reed grow only by water and can stand in water one block deep
- Sedge and bracken grow under trees, grasses and wildflowers in the open, blackberries in a ring along the crowns' edges
- Plants grow only on newly generated ground; chunks generated before do not get them

## Walking and driving through

- Walking through plants pushes the leaves and stems near you aside; they sway back after you pass
- Your path leaves footprints: the plants lie down the way you walked, rise halfway after about one in-game day and stand again about half a day later
- A wheel flattens the whole block; it starts to rise after about two in-game days
- Plants do not block your way
- <StatusTag s="test" /> Plants rustle as you walk through them, with three sounds for short grass, tall grass and brambles. Sneaking is quietest and sprinting loudest; they carry as far as your footsteps, and plants already flattened are quieter. Each rustle plays out before the next starts, so walking on does not restart it every step

## Near and far

- Within 24 m you see the whole plants; between 24 and 40 m they change clump by clump into simpler pictures, and farther away into simpler ones still
- Tall plants (cattail, reed, big bluestem, goldenrod, blackberry) are drawn at every distance; low ones disappear beyond about 120 m
- The simpler far pictures match the size of the plants up close

## Wind

- Plants sway in the wind; tall ones sway more, blackberry canes are stiff and sway less
- With the Aureveil shader pack, the pack sways them using AFL's plant data
